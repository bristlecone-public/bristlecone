# P1 — the frequency-weighted CPI

`pipelines/p1_salience.py` → tables `p1_ucc_freq`, `p1_item_salience`, `p1_index`,
`p1_sensitivity`, `p1_boot`, file `out/p1_salience.json`

The published CPI weights each item by **how much money households spend on it**. Owners'
equivalent rent is 25.8% of the basket because shelter is 25.8% of spending. This pipeline
rebuilds the same index weighting each item instead by **how often households record buying
it** — the CE Diary purchase-entry rate — and reports the difference. It is a descriptive
re-weighting exercise. Nothing here measures inflation "as experienced" or "as felt"; those
words appear nowhere on purpose.

> Naming note: the user-facing label is **frequency-weighted**. Table and column names keep
> the older word `salience` (`p1_item_salience.w_sal`, `p1_index.scheme = 'salience'`)
> because other pipelines read them.

## Part 1 — in plain language

### The five indexes

| Scheme (`p1_index.scheme`) | Label | What it weights by |
|---|---|---|
| `official_replica` | Official (expenditure-weighted) replica | December relative importance — our rebuild of the published CPI-U |
| `salience` | **Frequency-weighted CPI** | Weighted CE Diary purchase entries per consumer-unit-week |
| `blend50` | 50/50 expenditure + frequency | Half of each |
| `bill_salience` | **Bill-adjusted frequency-weighted CPI** | Frequency, with a one-bill-a-month floor under 17 recurring-bill items |
| `bill_blend50` | 50/50 expenditure + bill-adjusted frequency | Half of each |

### July 2026

| | YoY | ± BRR SE |
|---|---|---|
| Published CPI-U (NSA, all items) | **3.365%** | — |
| `official_replica` | 3.292% | — |
| `salience` (frequency-weighted) | **3.215%** | ±0.019 |
| `blend50` | 3.253% | ±0.009 |
| `bill_salience` | **3.088%** | ±0.016 |
| `bill_blend50` | 3.189% | ±0.008 |

Two earlier reference months:

| | Jun 2022 | Jun 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Published | 9.060% | 2.971% |
| `official_replica` | 9.131% | 2.900% |
| `salience` | **12.004%** ±0.046 | **1.156%** ±0.015 |
| `blend50` | 10.567% ±0.023 | 2.019% ±0.007 |
| `bill_salience` | 11.361% ±0.039 | 1.507% ±0.012 |
| `bill_blend50` | 10.246% ±0.019 | 2.195% ±0.006 |

The pattern is the story: because purchase frequency loads onto **groceries, restaurant
meals and fuel** and almost nothing else, the frequency-weighted index *overshoots badly*
in a food-and-energy shock (2022) and *undershoots* when shelter and services are carrying
inflation (2024, 2026). It is not a smoothed or "truer" version of CPI; it is a different,
much more concentrated basket.

### Why the raw frequency index is not enough — the bill problem

The CE Diary is a two-week purchase log. A household writes down the eggs, but it does not
write down its rent, its insurance premiums or its electricity bill — those come from the
separate CE Interview survey. So in the raw frequency weights:

| Item | Share of spending | Share of purchase entries |
|---|---|---|
| Owners' equivalent rent | 25.8% | 0.00% |
| Rent of primary residence | 8.0% | 0.15% |
| Electricity | 2.5% | 0.00% |
| Motor vehicle insurance | 2.8% | 0.20% |
| Health insurance | 0.9% | 0.00% |
| Wireless telephone service | 1.4% | 0.00% |
| Limited-service meals | 2.7% | **5.96%** |
| Other fresh vegetables | 0.3% | **4.39%** |
| Spices, seasonings, condiments, sauces | 0.3% | **3.94%** |

Nobody thinks rent is a thing you notice zero times a year. The **`bill_salience`** variant
puts a floor under a documented list of 17 recurring-bill items: each is treated as at
least **one purchase event per month** (1 / 4.345 entries per consumer-unit-week, since a
month is 4.345 weeks), *unless* the Diary already sees it more often. Nothing else changes.

The 17 items are rent, owners' equivalent rent, tenants'/household insurance, motor vehicle
insurance, health insurance, electricity, utility gas, water & sewerage, garbage & trash,
wireless phone, residential phone, internet, cable/satellite/streaming, college tuition,
elementary & high-school tuition, day care & preschool, and state motor-vehicle registration
& licence fees. The floor binds for **all 17** — none is seen monthly in the Diary — so each
ends up at exactly 0.663% of the July-2026 basket, and the group goes from **1.04% to 11.27%**
of total weight.

That *lowers* the July-2026 rate (3.215 → 3.088) rather than raising it, because the biggest
weight gains go to items that are currently falling: health insurance (−8.0% YoY), motor
vehicle insurance (−4.5%), wireless phone (−3.6%).

**This is an assumption, not a measurement.** "One bill a month" is a stipulation about
what a bill is; the Diary cannot confirm or refute it.

### How much do the arbitrary choices matter?

Three modelling choices could each have gone the other way. All three are rebuilt as
separate indexes in `p1_sensitivity`:

| Variant | Choice | Jul-2026 YoY | Gap vs replica |
|---|---|---|---|
| `diary_2022` | 2022 Diary frequencies for every year | 3.501 | +0.209 |
| `diary_pooled` | all three Diary years pooled | 3.308 | +0.016 |
| `split_equal` | multi-ELI UCCs split equally | 3.261 | −0.031 |
| `diary_2023` | 2023 Diary frequencies for every year | 3.261 | −0.031 |
| `split_exp` = `agg_item` = **headline** | year-matched Diary, expenditure split, item-level | 3.215 | −0.077 |
| `diary_2024` | 2024 Diary frequencies for every year | 3.215 | −0.077 |
| `agg_class` | frequency measured at expenditure-class level | 3.215 | −0.077 |

**Range of the gap across all 8 variants at 2026-07: 0.286 pp** (−0.077 to +0.209).

Reading that: the *specification* choices (how to split a UCC that maps to several items, at
what level of the item tree to measure frequency) barely matter — `agg_class` moves the
headline by 0.0004 pp, `split_equal` by 0.046 pp. **Which Diary year you use matters far
more** (0.286 pp between 2022 and 2024), because the frequency profile itself shifted after
2022. That is the honest uncertainty in this method, and it is roughly 15× the sampling
error below.

### Sampling error

The CE Diary is a sample of about 12,500 consumer-unit-weeks a year, so the frequency
weights are estimates. Re-computing everything under BLS's 44 replicate weights gives a
standard error on the frequency-vs-official gap of **±0.019 pp in July 2026** (±0.046 pp at
the 2022 peak, ±0.010–0.050 pp across the whole history). The frequency index's distance
from the official one — 0.08 pp now, 2.9 pp in mid-2022 — is far larger than its sampling
noise. Diary *sampling* is not the weak link; the Diary's *coverage* (the bill problem) and
the choice of Diary year are.

### History

Weights now run from **December 2017**, so every index starts January 2018 and every YoY
starts **January 2019** (previously 2022). October 2025 is absent everywhere — there was no
CPI release that month.

## Part 2 — technical appendix

### Frequency measure

For CE Diary year *y*, with consumer-unit-weeks *c* (one row per CU per diary week),
final weight *W<sub>c</sub>* = `FINLWT21`, and *n<sub>c,u</sub>* = the number of `expd`
purchase entries CU-week *c* recorded for UCC *u*:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;`entries_per_cuweek(u)` = Σ*<sub>c</sub> n<sub>c,u</sub> W<sub>c</sub>* / Σ*<sub>c</sub> W<sub>c</sub>*

stored in `p1_ucc_freq` alongside `spend_per_cuweek` and `share_buying` (weighted share of
CU-weeks with at least one entry for that UCC). Diary years 2022/2023/2024 give 12,709 /
12,389 / 12,507 CU-weeks and 414,553 / 396,930 / 416,271 expenditure rows.

### UCC → published item

The BLS Handbook Appendix 5 concordance (`raw/ce/ce-cpi-concordance.xlsx`, 624 rows, 507
UCCs) maps UCC → ELI; ELI → published item is `'SE' + ELI[:4]`, walking up to `[:3]` and
`[:2]` when the finer stratum is not published. **29 UCCs fan out to more than one ELI.**
Those are split across their target items in proportion to the targets' **expenditure
weight** (a proxy for BLS's unpublished special adjustment factors); a UCC whose targets all
have zero weight is split equally. `split_equal` is the same construction with every mapped
target given an equal share.

Coverage on the newest weight year: 78 Diary UCCs (4.9% of entries) have no concordance row
at all — mostly food-away-from-home meal-occasion codes and non-consumption items — and
99.4% of the concordance-mapped entry mass lands on a published leaf.

Everything downstream is linear in the UCC entry vector, so it is implemented as a matrix
`A` (leaf × UCC) applied once per weight year; the class-level variant is a second matrix
`C` with (`C f`)*<sub>i</sub>* = *w<sup>exp</sup><sub>i</sub>* / Σ*<sub>class</sub> w<sup>exp</sup>* · Σ*<sub>class</sub> f*,
where the class is `item_code[:4]` (e.g. `SEFA`). 179 leaves fall into 70 classes, 54 of them
with more than one leaf.

### Bill floor

`BILL_FREQ` = 1 / 4.345 = 0.2302 entries per CU-week, applied as
*f<sub>i</sub>* ← max(*f<sub>i</sub>*, `BILL_FREQ`) for *i* ∈ `BILL_ITEMS`, **after** any
class-level re-aggregation and **before** normalisation to 100. The item list is a module
constant with its exclusions documented inline: hotels, "purchase/subscription/rental of
video" and "recorded music and music subscriptions" (they mix subscriptions with one-off
purchases), parking fees, and episodic medical services are deliberately *not* bills.

### Index construction

Identical to the P0 replica so the two are comparable — a December-chained Laspeyres over
`cpi_weight WHERE is_leaf=1 AND matched=1`:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*I<sub>t</sub>* = *I*<sub>Dec(y)</sub> · Σ*<sub>i</sub> w<sub>i</sub> P<sub>it</sub>/P<sub>i,Dec(y)</sub>* / Σ*<sub>i</sub> w<sub>i</sub>*

chained through each December; the December-2017 base level is the published SA0 index, and
every subsequent December comes from our own chain. `dec_chained()` is kept as the
module-level entry point.

**Coverage gate.** A month is priced only if leaves carrying ≥ **90%** (`MIN_COV`) of the
scheme's weight have both a December base and a current index. This is what keeps
October 2025 out: only 11.3% of the official-replica weight (2.8% of the frequency weight)
has an Oct-2025 observation, and without the gate the month was priced off ~11 items,
producing a spurious level and YoY. The gate applies to every scheme, every sensitivity
variant and every bootstrap replicate; dropped months are logged and listed in
`method.excluded_months`.

### Bootstrap

CE PUMD supplies 44 balanced-repeated-replication weights `WTREP01..WTREP44`; a CU has a
value > 0 in the replicates it belongs to and **blank** in the others (blank → 0 here;
2.8 × 10⁵ blank cells per year, i.e. ~50% of each column, as expected for half-samples). The
BLS CE PUMD *Getting Started Guide* §6.3 "Estimating sampling error" gives

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;σ = √( Σ<sub>WTREP=1</sub><sup>44</sup> ( *X̄<sub>WTREP</sub>* − *X̄* )² ⁄ 44 )

i.e. **variance = (1/44) Σ (θ*<sub>r</sub>* − θ)²**, and that is the formula used here
(`bootstrap.formula` in the JSON).

> The task brief specified (4/44)·Σ, which is the Fay *k* = 0.5 convention. That does not
> apply to PUMD: its replicate weights are already **doubled** half-sample weights re-raked
> to the population total — median `WTREP01/FINLWT21` = 2.004, and Σ`WTREP`*<sub>r</sub>* =
> Σ`FINLWT21` to 4 decimal places for all 44 replicates — so no Fay factor belongs in the
> denominator. **Multiply every SE reported here by 2 to get the Fay-convention number.**

θ is the gap (scheme YoY − `official_replica` YoY) in percentage points; the official
replica does not depend on the Diary, so this is also the SE of the scheme's own YoY. The
whole chain is re-run per replicate: entries → UCC→item map → normalise → December-chained
Laspeyres. Results land in `p1_boot(scheme, ym, yoy, se)` for `salience`, `blend50`,
`bill_salience`, `bill_blend50`, and as `*_se` in the JSON series.

All three diary years carry a complete set of 44 replicate columns, so nothing was skipped;
the code logs a warning and falls back to `FINLWT21` (zero variance contribution) for any
year that is missing them.

### Weight back-fill to December 2017

`https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/relative-importance/{y}.htm` only exists from 2020. The
pre-2020 tables live in **`ri-archive-2010-2019.zip`** on the same directory as fixed-width
`*.txt` files (there are also `ri-archive-2000-2009.zip`, `1990-1999`, `1987-1989`, and
`historical-relative-importance-1947-1986.xlsx`, all unused so far).

`ri_txt_to_html()` converts `2017.txt`/`2018.txt`/`2019.txt` into the minimal HTML shape
`p0_fetch.load_weights` already parses (dot-leader names that wrap over up to three
lines, `nn.nnn`/`.nnn` CPI-U and CPI-W columns, and the "Special aggregate indexes" marker),
stages it at `raw/weights/{y}.htm`, then calls **`p0_fetch.load_weights(con, years=[...])`
unmodified** behind a local-first `fetch` shim, so the name→code matching, the level and the
leaf detection are byte-identical to the 2020+ vintages. 16 item titles changed between 2017
and today's `cu.item` (e.g. "Cable and satellite television service" →
"Cable, satellite, and live streaming television service", "Child care and nursery school" →
"Day care and preschool"); those are rewritten in *our staged HTML* via `RI_RENAME`, never in
p0. Result: 321 rows and 182 leaves per year, leaf weight 97.68 / 97.66 / 97.56 — in line
with 2020–2025 (95.58–97.65) — with only "unsampled …" strata left unmatched.

Validation of the extension: `official_replica` YoY vs published CPI-U YoY, 90 months
2019-01 … 2026-07, mean −0.038 pp, max |diff| 0.209 pp. By year:

| | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean | −0.036 | −0.031 | +0.103 | +0.024 | −0.159 | −0.071 | −0.085 | −0.069 |
| max abs | 0.048 | 0.095 | 0.156 | 0.119 | **0.209** | 0.122 | 0.126 | 0.116 |

The back-filled years are the *best*-tracking ones, which is the evidence that the archive
parse is right.

### Side effects on shared tables

`extend_weights()` writes `cpi_weight` rows for weight years 2017–2019 (P0's table). This is
intentional and idempotent — it is skipped when those years are already present, and P0's
February `--weights --force` refresh only touches 2020+. The next `p0_fetch.py`
`build_item_month()` will therefore start populating `cpi_item_month.weight` for 2018–2020
months, which previously were null. No other agent's file was modified.

### Known limitations

- **The bill floor is a stipulation.** One entry per month for 17 items is an assumption
  about what a recurring bill is. Every one of the 17 ends up with the identical weight,
  which is crude — it says a rent payment and a garbage bill are equally salient events.
- **Purchase-entry counts partly measure classification depth.** An item BLS splits into
  several UCCs accumulates more entries than an equally common item recorded as one. The
  `agg_class` variant measures this and finds it small at the aggregate (0.0004 pp on the
  July-2026 headline) — but that is an *aggregate* result, and it can still be large for an
  individual item.
- **Which Diary year you use dominates.** 0.286 pp between the 2022-only and 2024-only
  variants at the latest month, against a 0.019 pp sampling SE. Frequency profiles are not
  stable across years.
- **Frequency ≠ salience ≠ perception.** No survey of what households notice is used
  anywhere in this pipeline. The `salience` scheme name is legacy.
- **Diary years stop at 2024**, so weight years 2025 (and 2017–2022, at the other end) reuse
  the nearest available Diary year's frequency profile. `p1_item_salience` is keyed by
  `(diary_year, item_code)`, so weight years sharing a diary year overwrite each other there;
  `p1_index` and `p1_sensitivity` are unaffected.
- **~5% of Diary entries have no concordance row** and are dropped, and the leaf universe
  itself covers ~97% of the CPI (the rest is unsampled strata with no index, renormalised
  away) — the same treatment as P0.
- **Pre-2021 CPI item composition differs** from today's. The 2017–2019 leaf sets carry three
  items that no longer exist as published leaves (`SEEB04`, `SEHB01`, `SEMD03`).

### Re-running

```
$CPI_ROOT/p1_salience.py [--years=22,23,24] [--no-boot] [--no-extend] [--force-extend]
```
Idempotent, ~6 s end to end including the 44-replicate bootstrap, wired into
`run_monthly.sh` after P0 with no flags.
