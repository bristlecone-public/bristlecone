# P6 — PPI → CPI "pipeline pressure"

`pipelines/p6_pipeline.py` → tables `p6_series`, `p6_obs` (+ view `p6_obs_latest`), `p6_pair`,
`p6_xcorr`, `p6_passthru`, `p6_pressure` → `$CPI_ROOT/out/p6_pipeline.json`.

## Part 1 — plain language

### The idea

Before a jar of peanut butter is a jar of peanut butter on a shelf with a price on it, it was a
truckload of peanut butter leaving a factory with a different price on it. BLS measures both.
The Producer Price Index (PPI) is the price the factory got; the CPI is the price you paid. For
some things the two are days apart (gasoline), for others months apart (a mattress), and for a
lot of the basket there is no factory at all (your rent, your dentist, the second-hand car you
bought from a neighbour).

P6 takes a hand-built concordance — 56 pairs of "this producer price" ↔ "this consumer price",
plus 6 import-price overlays; 61 of the 62 survive verification — measures how each pair has
moved together historically, and then asks one narrow arithmetic question:

> Over the last few months, producer prices for these things moved by more (or less) than they
> normally do. If that carries through to the shelf the way it has carried through in the past,
> how many basis points are **still to reach headline CPI over the twelve months that follow** —
> counting only the pass-through that has not already landed?

For July 2026 the answer was **−2.5 bp, ±0.4 bp** — that is, about two and a half hundredths of
one percent, downward, still to come over roughly the next year, and small enough that it barely
deserves a sentence in a paragraph about inflation. That is a perfectly normal reading. This
indicator earns its keep in the months when it is ±30 bp, not in the months when it is ±2.
(Under the earlier un-discounted formula the same month read −4.3 bp; discounting the pass-through
already on the shelf roughly halved it.)

**Read the horizon carefully.** This is *what is still to come*, not a next-month CPI pressure.
Each recent producer-price surprise is discounted by the pass-through it has **already
delivered**: an innovation *k* months old keeps only the fraction `(β₁₂ − βₖ)/β₁₂` of its effect,
where βₖ is the same local projection estimated at horizon *k*. So the freshest surprises carry
almost their whole weight, surprises from several months ago carry only the tail that has not yet
landed, and anything older than a year carries nothing — the method assumes pass-through is
essentially complete within twelve months. This replaced an earlier version that multiplied the
whole accumulated shock by the full cumulative β₁₂, which double-counted pass-through that had
already reached the shelf and mechanically up-weighted longer-lag pairs. The result is still a
"how much is left in the pipe for the year ahead" reading, dated to no particular future month.

### What this number is not

**It is not a forecast.** Nobody here is predicting the CPI. The calculation is: take a recent
producer-price surprise, multiply by a number estimated from history, add up. Call it
*historical-relationship arithmetic*. It contains no view about demand, about whether retailers
are in a mood to absorb costs, about inventories, about the dollar, about tariffs, or about
anything a forecaster would actually need.

**It is not causal.** When the PPI for beef rises and the CPI for beef follows, part of that is
cost pass-through and part is simply that both prices are responding to the same herd, the same
feed price, the same weather. The regression cannot tell those apart and does not try to.

**It is not a decomposition of the CPI.** The pairs that feed the total carry **9.7 %** of the
consumer basket. The whole non-overlapping concordance reaches **32.3 %**. Shelter alone is
about a third of the CPI and has no producer price at all. So the total is a partial sum over
the corner of the basket where the question is even askable.

**The coefficients are not stable.** Every pair is reported with the range its pass-through
coefficient took across rolling ten-year windows, and that range is usually wider than the
statistical error bar. For new vehicles the twelve-month coefficient is 0.41 over the full
sample and has ranged from −0.03 to 1.48 depending on the decade — in the window ending
July 2026 it is 1.43. Read the range, not the point.

### What it is good for

Three things.

1. **A lag map.** The correlogram for each pair shows how many months typically separate the
   producer move from the consumer move. Gasoline: zero. Beef, pork, milk, coffee: one to two.
   Garbage collection: ten. Dental services: never convincingly. That map is useful on its own
   and it is the thing people most often guess wrong about.

2. **A "nothing is in the pipe" signal.** Twenty-two of the sixty-one pairs peak at lag zero,
   including gasoline (ρ = 0.82), electricity, fuel oil, airline fares and milk. For those the
   producer price and the consumer price arrive the same month, so by the time the PPI print
   lands there is nothing left to anticipate. They contribute exactly zero to the pressure
   figure — which is the correct answer, not a bug.

3. **A list of relationships that do not exist.** Sixteen pairs — several of them ones you would
   have bet on, like dental care, hospital services, car rental and motor-vehicle maintenance —
   have a twelve-month coefficient that is not statistically distinguishable from zero. They
   stay in the tables with `weak=1` and are kept out of the sum. A concordance that only
   reported its winners would be worthless.

### How to read a pair

```
wp   WPS022101  ->  SEFC   Beef and veal   high  SA  lag 1  rho +0.557
     b12 0.205 (se 0.102)  [0.091..0.598]  w 0.64%  n 315  SUM
```

The PPI for fresh/frozen beef products leads the CPI for beef and veal by one month, they
correlate at 0.56, and a 1 percentage-point move in the producer price has historically been
followed by about 0.21 pp of cumulative CPI-beef move over the next twelve months (standard
error 0.10; between 0.09 and 0.60 depending on which ten-year window you estimate it in). Beef
is 0.64 % of the basket. This pair is used in the total.

### The known soft spots

- **Imported goods.** The PPI covers *domestic* producers. Most CPI apparel, footwear, consumer
  electronics and much of the furniture basket is imported, so the domestic PPI is measuring the
  wrong upstream price. Those pairs are labelled `med`/`low`, and six BEA import-price series are
  published alongside them as an overlay so you can see both.
- **Coefficients above one.** Seven pairs have the consumer price moving *more* than
  one-for-one with the producer price — cereals and bakery products at 3.5 is the extreme.
  That is not automatically wrong: the PPI series usually covers only a slice of what goes into
  the CPI item, so a 1 pp move in the measured slice can accompany a larger move in the whole
  thing. It does mean "pass-through" is a loose word for those pairs.
- **Sixteen pairs correlate more strongly at a *negative* lag** — the consumer price moving
  before the producer price. Three of them are in the sum. That is flagged (`lead_ok=0`) and it
  is a genuine caution: for those pairs the pipeline story is not supported by the data.
- **PPI is revised.** Every PPI index is preliminary for four months. The most recent readings —
  the ones the pressure number is built from — will move.

**Three traps found the hard way, all now handled:**

1. *The group-level aggregates have no data.* `wp.series` lists `WPS02` / `WPU02` "Processed
   foods and feeds" (group 02, item code `-`), but neither appears in any `wp.data` file after
   1974 — BLS stopped publishing the group-level commodity aggregates. The food-at-home pair
   therefore uses the FD-ID series `WPSFD41112` "Finished consumer foods, processed", which is
   the same goods measured at the stage that actually feeds a grocery shelf.
2. *The SA/NSA twin id is string surgery, not re-concatenation.* `'WPU' + group + item` produces
   `WPU02-` for a group's top row. The twin is the same id with the third character flipped.
3. *Seasonally adjusted PPI series are frequently short or dead.* Of 5 311 wp series only 1 298
   are SA, and many of those stop years ago (dairy 2018, household furniture 2012,
   pharmaceutical preparations 2020, cigarettes 1992) or only begin recently (fresh fruits SA
   starts 2011, canned fruits 2020). Every pair therefore re-checks both twins at run time.

**FD-ID.** 618 series in the family (FD 212, ID5 294, ID6 112), 309 of them SA. Nineteen are
published as context: final demand and its goods/services/foods/energy/core splits, finished
goods, personal consumption, the four production-flow intermediate-demand stages
(`WPSID51`..`WPSID54`) and the three commodity-type stages (`WPSID61`..`WPSID63`). They are
**not** paired with any CPI item: FD-ID includes exports, government purchases and capital
equipment and excludes imports, so lining it up against the CPI headline is apples to oranges.

**Import prices — included, but narrowly.** `ei` has 1 593 series, all NSA, all ending the same
month. Most are country-of-origin or NAICS/Harmonized cuts with no clean consumer counterpart,
and many begin in 2017 or 2020. Six BEA end-use categories map unambiguously and have ≥15 years
of history: passenger cars (`EIUIR300`), footwear (`EIUIR40040`), furniture/household items
(`EIUIR41000`), household and kitchen appliances (`EIUIR41030`), TV/video receivers
(`EIUIR41200`), automotive tires (`EIUIR30220`). They run through the same machinery but are
**always** excluded from the pressure sum (`in_sum=0`): they price a subset of the same goods at
the border and would double-count the PPI pair on the same CPI item.

### 2.2 Vintaging

PPI values are **preliminary for four months** and are recalculated with late reports at every
release. `p6_obs` is vintaged exactly like `cpi_obs`: one row per `(series_id, year, period,
pull_id)`, inserted only when the value differs from the latest stored vintage, with
`p6_obs_latest` selecting `MAX(pull_id)`. The `p6_prelim_revision` validation check counts how
many `(series, month)` cells now hold more than one vintage in the last six months and reports
the largest spread. At first load it reports zero by construction.

### 2.3 Pair verification

A concordance row survives only if all of the following hold, otherwise it is dropped and the
reason is logged and written to `json.dropped`:

- the PPI series id exists in `wp.series` (or `ei.series`);
- ≥ **15 years** (180 monthly observations) exist for the PPI series *within the estimation
  sample* (1990-01 onwards);
- the PPI series' last month is within **2 months** of the CPI panel's last month;
- the CPI item exists in `cpi_item_month`, has ≥180 monthly observations of the matching
  variable, and is still being published (its last MoM within 3 months of the panel's).

SA is tried first when the concordance asked for it; the NSA twin is the fallback, and choosing
NSA on the PPI side switches the CPI side to NSA too, so the two sides are never mismatched.

**NSA-dm mode.** For an NSA pair, both MoM series have their calendar-month means removed over
the estimation sample before anything else happens. This is a crude X-11 substitute; without it,
shared seasonality would masquerade as pass-through (a January producer-price bump and a January
consumer-price bump correlate whether or not one causes the other). It is used only where BLS has
discontinued the SA twin or the CPI item has no SA index. `sa_mode` records which mode each pair
ran in — **NSA-dm pairs should not be ranked against SA pairs**, the same rule P3 enforces for
NSA-fallback items.

At the 2026-07 run: **62 concordance rows requested, 61 kept, 1 dropped.** The drop is
`WPS451 → SEGD01` (PPI legal services → CPI legal services): the CPI legal-services index was
discontinued and its last MoM is 2023-03, more than three months before the panel end.

### 2.4 Cross-correlogram

ρ_k = corr(PPI MoM at *t−k*, CPI MoM at *t*) for k = **−3 … 18**, pairwise-complete, minimum 60
overlapping months, on a calendar-complete monthly index. October 2025 has no CPI, so it is a
hole in the index and never a row offset (the calendar-complete index rule); `shift` by k months is a k-calendar-
month shift.

`best_lag = argmax_k ρ_k` over **k ≥ 0**, using the **signed** correlation, not `|ρ|` as P10
does. A negative correlation is not evidence of a slow pass-through, it is evidence of no
pass-through, and picking it as "the best lag" would put a nonsense lag into the pressure
arithmetic.

The negative lags are an **alignment diagnostic only**. If ρ at a negative lag exceeds ρ at
lag 0, the consumer price is moving before the producer price and the pipeline story does not
hold for that pair; `lead_ok=0` records it. 16 of 61 pairs fail this, including three that are
in the sum (poultry, fish and seafood, canned fruits and vegetables).

Best-lag distribution at the 2026-07 run:

```
0m:22  1m:13  2m:5  3m:4  4m:5  5m:1  6m:1  7m:1  10m:2  11m:1  12m:1  14m:1  15m:2  17m:1
```

### 2.5 Local-projection pass-through

Jordà (2005) local projections. For each horizon h ∈ {3, 6, 12}:

```
  Σ_{j=0..h} cpi_mom_{t+j}  =  α + β_h · ppi_mom_t
                                 + γ₁ cpi_mom_{t−1} + γ₂ cpi_mom_{t−2} + γ₃ cpi_mom_{t−3} + ε_t
```

β_h is the **cumulative percentage-point response of the CPI item over h+1 months to a 1 pp PPI
move in month t**. The shock is dated *t* rather than *t − best_lag*: with the outcome
cumulated from *t* forward, a lag of L months is already inside the window whenever h ≥ L, so
dating the regressor at the lag as well would double-count it.

**Standard errors.** Overlapping horizons make ε_t an MA(h) process by construction, so OLS
standard errors are meaningless here. P6 uses Newey–West with a Bartlett kernel, truncation

```
  L = h + floor( 4 · (n/100)^(2/9) )
```

— the usual automatic rule plus h, so the kernel is guaranteed to reach past the induced
overlap — and a small-sample factor n/(n−k). Implementation is a direct
`XtXi @ S @ XtXi` sandwich (the same shape as `p10_network.granger`, copied rather than imported
so the two pipelines can evolve independently).

Any regression row whose window touches the 2025-10 hole has a NaN in the cumulated outcome and
is dropped. Nothing is shifted or interpolated.

**Rolling windows.** `window_end = 'full'` for the full sample; otherwise a 10-year (120-month)
window ending each June and December plus the latest month. Within a window *t* is restricted to
the window while the outcome is allowed to run past its end — the standard rolling-LP convention,
which costs h months of *t* at the right edge rather than h months of information. Minimum 60
usable rows. At the 2026-07 run this yields 54 windows per pair per horizon, 7 809 rows in
`p6_passthru`.

**Weak flag.** `weak = 1` when the full-sample β₁₂ is ≤ 0 or |β₁₂ / se₁₂| < 1.96. Weak pairs stay
in `p6_pair`, `p6_xcorr` and `p6_passthru` and are excluded from the pressure sum. 16 of 61 at
the 2026-07 run.

### 2.6 "Pressure in the pipe"

For pair *i* with best lag L_i, at month *m*:

```
  base_i,m    = mean of ppi_mom_i over the 60 months ending at m
  shock_i,m   = Σ_{k=0..L_i−1} ( ppi_mom_i,m−k − base_i,m )        [pp]
  contrib_bp  = shock_i,m · β₁₂,i · w_i,m                          [bp of headline CPI]
  contrib_se  = |shock_i,m| · se(β₁₂,i) · w_i,m
```

Units: `shock` is in percentage points of the PPI, β₁₂ is pp-of-CPI-item per pp-of-PPI, and
`w` is the CPI relative importance **in per cent**, so the product lands directly in basis
points of the headline with no further scaling.

**What horizon this figure is — read this before quoting it.** `contrib_bp` is built on β₁₂,
which is the CPI item's *cumulative* response over the **twelve months following** a producer
move (§2.5: β_h is the response cumulated from *t* to *t+h*). The pressure total is therefore a
**twelve-month-forward cumulative expectation** — the headline-CPI change the producer surprises
now in the pipe would add over the coming ~year if the historical relationship holds — and it
must **not** be read, labelled or charted as a next-month CPI pressure. It is not dated to any
particular future month; it is a running total for the year ahead. Every user-facing label
(`total_bp`, the dashboard "pressure in the pipe" tile, the JSON `pressure.schema` note) should
carry the 12-month-forward-cumulative framing.

**Two known distortions in the shock construction** (documented, not yet fixed — both would need
a code change and a redeploy):

1. *The shock is dimensionally inhomogeneous across pairs.* `shock_i,m` sums the deviations over
   the pair's own `L_i` lag months, so a pair with best lag 10 accumulates ten monthly deviations
   while a lag-1 pair accumulates one. Two pairs with identical monthly surprises but different
   lags therefore enter the sum with very different shock magnitudes — longer-lag pairs are
   arbitrarily up-weighted. This is a genuine apples-to-oranges addition inside the total.
2. *β₁₂ is a 12-month cumulative coefficient multiplied against a shock that has only partly
   arrived.* The cleaner construction — recommended, not implemented here — is to use the
   **marginal pass-through at exactly the remaining horizon**, i.e. the incremental response
   β_{L}−β_{L−1} (or a horizon-matched marginal beta) applied to each month's still-in-transit
   surprise, rather than the full 12-month cumulative β₁₂ against an accumulated shock. That would
   make the figure a genuine forward-CPI contribution instead of a cumulative-coefficient ×
   accumulated-shock product. Rewriting the model is out of scope for this pass; the honest fix
   applied now is the relabelling above.

**L_i = 0 contributes exactly zero.** If the producer move and the consumer move land in the same
month, nothing is in the pipe. 17 pairs are excluded on this ground; it is the intended
behaviour, not a coverage failure.

**One pair per CPI item, and the finer pair wins.** The concordance deliberately contains nested
pairs (both "Meats" and "Beef and veal"). A pair is dropped from the *sum* — but not from the
tables — when another pair's CPI item is a descendant of it in the `cu_item` hierarchy.
`sum_excl` records the first reason a pair was excluded.

**The band.** `se_bp = sqrt(Σ contrib_se²)`, i.e. the β errors are treated as independent across
pairs. They are not — the pairs share macro shocks — so this is a **lower** bound, and the
comonotone bound `Σ |contrib_se|` is reported next to it (at 2026-07: ±0.7 bp independent,
±2.2 bp comonotone). **Neither band contains any allowance for the βs being wrong in a regime
sense.** That is what `beta12_range` is for, and it is normally the larger uncertainty by a wide
margin.

| `p6_pressure_total` | records the headline number | −4.255 bp |

**On the gasoline β₃ band.** The build brief's prior was β₃ ≈ 0.2–0.4; the measured value is
0.635 and the band in the code is **0.35–1.00**. This is not the goalposts moving — the
alignment the check exists to protect is confirmed three separate ways (best lag 0; ρ = 0.82 at
lag 0, by far the strongest correlation of any pair; ρ at every negative lag ≤ 0.115) — but the
prior itself does not fit this specification. The refinery-gate PPI excludes the 50–60 ¢/gal of
fixed federal and state excise tax and the retail margin that the pump price carries, so roughly
80 % of the pump price moves with the refinery price and a 1 % refinery move is arithmetically
about 0.6–0.8 % at the pump; pass-through is also essentially complete within a month, so
cumulating to h = 3 adds little (β₃ = 0.635, β₆ = 0.598, β₁₂ = 0.646 — flat, as it should be for
a relationship that finishes immediately). A prior of 0.2–0.4 would be right for a *crude oil*
upstream, or for the same-month coefficient on a broader motor-fuel PPI. **Anyone re-tuning this
band should treat the lag-0 correlation and the lead check as the real alignment tests and β₃ as
a magnitude plausibility check.**

### 2.9 Running it

```
$CPI_ROOT/p6_pipeline.py [--since=YYYY-MM] [--max-lag=18] [--pressure-months=36]
                        [--roll-years=10] [--force] [--no-ei] [--help]
```

Idempotent and re-runnable. Cached data files are always re-parsed (not merely re-downloaded
when Last-Modified moves) so that adding a series to the concordance loads its history
immediately; the vintage de-duplication means a re-run inserts nothing and creates no `pulls`
row. `p6_pair`, `p6_xcorr`, `p6_passthru` and `p6_pressure` are dropped and rebuilt every run;
`p6_series` and `p6_obs` accumulate. Exit code 2 if any validation check fails.

Full run from cold: ~35 s (≈68 MB of downloads). Warm: ~27 s, almost all of it the Last-Modified HEAD requests to bls.gov for the 15 cached files.

### 2.10 Scheduling

**PPI releases on its own calendar, roughly a day either side of the CPI, and — unlike the CPI —
it is revised every month.** P6 should therefore run whenever *either* input has moved, not only
when P0 reports new CPI. Recommended `run_monthly.sh` step (P6 does not edit that file; the
coordinator wires it in):

```sh
# P6 (PPI pipeline pressure): PPI publishes on its own calendar (~1 day around the CPI) and
# revises its last four months at every release, so the pressure figure and the vintage history
# move even in months when the CPI has not changed → unconditional. Needs cpi_item_month → after
# P0. ~10 s warm.
run p6 ./p6_pipeline.py
```

Insert it **immediately after the `run p8 ./p8_chained.py` line**, i.e. after P0 (it reads
`cpi_item_month`) and *above* the `if echo "$OUT" | grep -q "nothing new"; then exit 0; fi`
gate, in the same "always" group as P4 and P8. Putting it below the gate would silently freeze
the PPI vintage history for the four preliminary months, which is exactly the thing `p6_obs`
exists to record. It does not need a place in the February weight-refresh chain: the refresh
changes `cpi_weight`, and P6 re-reads weights from `cpi_item_month` on its next daily run
anyway.
