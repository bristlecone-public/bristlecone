# P2 — the shape of inflation across items

`pipelines/p2_distribution.py` → tables `p2_dist`, `p2_varshare`, file `out/p2_distribution.json`

Headline CPI is one number. It is built from about 180 published leaf items whose annual
rates in July 2026 ran from **eggs at −25.7%** to **fuel oil at +39.1%**. P2 measures the
*shape* of that cross-section every month, and asks which items actually move the
aggregate around. Everything here is descriptive — it says what the published indexes did,
never why.

---

## Part 1 — the statistics, in plain language

Every statistic is **weighted by how much of the household budget the item represents**
(December relative importance), so a 25% swing in eggs (0.13% of spending) counts far less
than a 3% swing in rent. All of them are computed twice: over **all** leaf items and over
**ex-shelter**, which drops rent, owners' equivalent rent, lodging away from home and
tenants' insurance — about a third of the basket.

| Statistic | What it answers | Reading it |
|---|---|---|
| **Weighted mean** | "What is inflation?" | This is essentially the headline rate. One huge item can carry it. |
| **Median** | "What is the *typical* item doing?" | Half the basket (by spending) is rising faster, half slower. Ignores extremes entirely. |
| **10th / 25th / 75th / 90th percentile** | "How spread out is it?" | In July 2026 the 10th percentile was −1.9% and the 90th was +5.2%: the basket is not moving together. A widening gap means the average is describing fewer and fewer real prices. |
| **Trimmed mean (8% / 16%)** | "What's left after throwing out the drama?" | Cut the cheapest-moving and dearest-moving items — 8% or 16% of *spending* off each end — and average the rest. This is the standard "underlying inflation" measure; the Cleveland Fed publishes a 16% version. |
| **Skewness** | "Which tail is longer?" | Positive = a few items running very hot pull the average above the typical item. Negative = a few items collapsing pull it below. |
| **Excess kurtosis** | "How outlier-driven is the month?" | High values mean the average is hostage to a handful of items. Zero is a normal bell curve; CPI months routinely run 5–80. |
| **Share of weight above 2 / 4 / 6%** | "How much of the basket is actually hot?" | July 2026: 76% of the basket rose more than 2%, 21% more than 4%, 8.6% more than 6%. |
| **Share of weight below 0%** | "How much is actually falling?" | July 2026: 15.2% of the basket — outright deflation is normal even in an inflationary month. |

Each statistic is also computed on two other views of the same item: the **3-month
annualised** SA change (`ann3m`, faster-moving) and the **1-month** SA change (`mom_sa`,
noisiest). The 12-month `yoy` view is the one to show a consumer.

### "Who moves headline"

The last block answers a different question: over the last 24 or 60 months, whose ups and
downs actually **moved the total around**? Each item gets a share of the variance of the
monthly headline, and the shares add to exactly 100%.

Over the 24 months to July 2026, **gasoline alone accounts for 74% of the month-to-month
variance** of headline SA inflation while carrying only 2.9% of the basket. Owners'
equivalent rent, at 25.2% of the basket — nine times gasoline's weight — accounts for 2.3%.
That is the single most useful fact in P2: the item that determines *the level* of
inflation is almost never the item that determines *the monthly headline surprise*.

A negative share means the item consistently moved against the total, damping it.

## Part 2 — technical appendix

### Universe

Leaves are `cpi_weight WHERE is_leaf=1 AND matched=1` for `weight_year = calendar_year − 1`
(the December relative importance of year *Y* applies to the months of *Y*+1), joined to
`cpi_item_month`. That is 179 items covering ~97% of the CPI-U; the missing ~3% is
unsampled strata with no published index and is renormalised away.

`universe = 'exshelter'` drops `SEHA`, `SEHC01` and every leaf whose `cu_item` ancestry
contains `SAH1` — in practice `SEHA`, `SEHB02`, `SEHC01`, `SEHD`, 34.4% of the 2025 weight.

Weights are renormalised each month over the items that actually have a value for that
variable, so the effective universe differs by variable:

| variable | items (2026-07) | basket covered |
|---|---|---|
| `yoy` (NSA, 12-month) | 175 | 96.5% |
| `ann3m` (SA, 3-month annualised) | 141 | 85.9% |
| `mom_sa` (SA, 1-month) | 142 | 85.9% |

Seasonally adjusted indexes exist for fewer strata than NSA indexes, so every SA-based
statistic describes a smaller basket than the YoY ones. A month is skipped entirely if the
items with a value cover less than 60% of the basket (`MIN_COVW`) — this is what removes
October and November 2025 from the SA statistics, where only 10.1% of the basket has an SA
monthly change.

### Formulas

With items *i*, values *x<sub>i</sub>* and normalised weights *w<sub>i</sub>* (Σ*w* = 1),
sorted ascending by *x*:

- **mean** μ = Σ *w<sub>i</sub> x<sub>i</sub>*; central moments *m<sub>k</sub>* = Σ *w<sub>i</sub>*(*x<sub>i</sub>* − μ)<sup>*k*</sup>
- **skewness** = *m*<sub>3</sub> / *m*<sub>2</sub><sup>3/2</sup>; **excess kurtosis** = *m*<sub>4</sub> / *m*<sub>2</sub><sup>2</sup> − 3
- **percentile** *p*: the value of the first item at which the cumulative weight reaches
  *p* (step definition, no interpolation) — the same convention the Cleveland Fed uses for
  its median CPI
- **trimmed mean** at α: each item keeps the overlap of its weight interval
  [*W<sub>i</sub>* − *w<sub>i</sub>*, *W<sub>i</sub>*] with [α, 1 − α] (so boundary items are
  split, and the estimator is continuous in α), then a weighted mean over the retained
  weight. α = 0.08 and 0.16 per tail.
- **tail shares** = 100 × Σ *w<sub>i</sub>* over items past the threshold (percent of the
  *covered* basket, not of 100% of consumer spending)

**Variance decomposition.** Let *x<sub>it</sub>* be item SA MoM and *w<sub>it</sub>* its
renormalised weight, and define the contribution *c<sub>it</sub>* = *w<sub>it</sub> x<sub>it</sub>*
(percentage points). Then *X<sub>t</sub>* = Σ*<sub>i</sub> c<sub>it</sub>* is our replica of
headline SA MoM, and exactly

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Var(*X*) = Σ*<sub>i</sub>* Cov(*c<sub>i</sub>*, *X*)&nbsp;&nbsp;⟹&nbsp;&nbsp;
share*<sub>i</sub>* = Cov(*c<sub>i</sub>*, *X*) / Var(*X*), Σ*<sub>i</sub>* share*<sub>i</sub>* = 1.

This is the usual *w<sub>i</sub>* Cov(*x<sub>i</sub>*, *X*) / Var(*X*) with the weight folded
into *c*, which keeps the identity exact even though the weights change each January. An
item absent in a month contributes a true zero (it is not in *X<sub>t</sub>* either), so
zero-filling does not break the identity. Windows are 24 and 60 calendar months ending at
`month`; months with no CPI are dropped, never shifted, and a window needs ≥90% of its
months present.

### Validation (run 2026-08-19, data through 2026-07)

1. **Weighted mean of leaf YoY vs published SA0 YoY**, last 6 months — tolerance 0.2pp:
   `−0.012, −0.020, −0.100, −0.078, −0.116, −0.090` pp for 2026-02 … 2026-07. All pass.
   The residual is the fixed-weight approximation: BLS updates relative importances by the
   relative price change through the year, we hold December weights flat.
2. **Σ share<sub>i</sub> = 1** for both windows: 1.000000000 (22 of 24 months, 58 of 60
   months present after the Oct/Nov-2025 gap).
3. **Replica SA MoM vs published SA0 MoM**, 2000-01 onwards: corr 0.972, mean absolute
   difference 0.053pp, bias +0.022pp over 317 months. *X* is not the published series —
   it omits the strata without SA indexes — so the decomposition describes *X*, not SA0
   exactly.
4. **Cleveland Fed 16% trimmed-mean CPI** (`trim_revised.csv` from
   clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/median-cpi), 12-month rate, last 12 months. Our
   YoY-cross-section trimmed mean runs −0.13 to +0.34pp against theirs; our monthly SA
   trimmed means compounded over 12 months (the like-for-like construction) run +0.04 to
   +0.27pp. Expected: they trim ~45 aggregated components, we trim 142–175 leaves, and a
   finer partition trims less spending per unit of α.

All four checks are also written to the shared `validation` table with `p2_` check names.

### Known limitations

- **Owners' equivalent rent dominates every weighted moment.** At 25.2% of the basket
  (26.2% of the covered weight), OER alone spans the 41.7th to the 67.8th percentile of the
  July-2026 cross-section, so it *is* the weighted median in 46 of the 138 months since 2015
  — including all seven months of 2026 — and the median and p75 can collapse onto the same
  item (they did for `mom_sa` in July 2026, both 0.261). This is why the `exshelter`
  universe exists, and why the median should always be read next to the percentile spread.
- **Fixed December weights.** Within a year the weights never move, so a month where
  gasoline prices double still uses January's gasoline weight. This is a deliberate,
  documented approximation (see check 1) and it makes the P2 statistics comparable to the
  P0/P1 December-chained Laspeyres replica.
- **Weights only exist from December 2020** in `cpi_weight` (weight years 2020–2025). Months
  before 2021 reuse the oldest available vintage; those rows carry `weight_exact = 0` and
  are a genuinely *fixed-weight* historical cross-section, not the weights of the day. The
  item composition of the basket also changed over that period. Treat pre-2021 history as
  indicative.
- **SA statistics cover a smaller basket than YoY statistics** (86% vs 96.5%), and the
  variance decomposition inherits that. Items with no SA index — a handful of small strata —
  can never appear as variance contributors.
- **October 2025 has no CPI.** Every lag is calendar-aligned, so November 2025 MoM and
  January 2026 3-month-annualised are missing by design. The 12-month Cleveland comparison
  geometrically annualises the 10 or 11 months that exist rather than dropping the window.
- **Trimmed means are not monotone in α.** In July 2026 `trim08` = 2.75 but `trim16` = 2.95,
  because 15.2% of the basket was falling: widening the trim removes most of the negative
  tail. This is correct behaviour, not a bug, but it means the two trims are two different
  descriptions, not a robustness ladder.
- **A large variance share is not causation.** It means the item's contribution co-moved
  with the total over the window. Gasoline's 74% share reflects its volatility, not any
  claim about what drives inflation.

### Re-running

```
$CPI_ROOT/p2_distribution.py [--since YYYY-MM] [--varshare-since YYYY-MM] [--no-cleveland]
```
Idempotent (both tables are rebuilt), ~50s end to end, and wired into `run_monthly.sh`
after P1. Exit code 2 means a validation check fell outside tolerance; the tables and JSON
are still written.
