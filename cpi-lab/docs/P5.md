# P5 — CPI instrument health / data quality

P5 measures **how the CPI is being collected**, not what it says. It is a check on the instrument:
how much of the price data is actually observed, how much is imputed, by what method, how precise the
published changes are, and what BLS has publicly announced about its own collection.

Code: `pipelines/p5_quality.py` (deployed `$CPI_ROOT/p5_quality.py`) · Output: `$CPI_ROOT/out/p5_quality.json`
Runs from `run_monthly.sh` on every daily poll, before P0, tolerant of any single source failing.

---

## The distinction that everything here depends on

**The imputation tables are a distribution *among imputations*, not an imputation rate.**

BLS says this in plain text on the source page: *"They do not represent an overall imputation rate for
each survey."* When the July 2026 table shows **different cell 37%**, that means:

> Of the price observations BLS had to impute in July 2026, 37% were imputed using a *different cell*
> (same item category, wider geography) instead of the preferred *home cell* (same item, same area).

It does **not** mean 37% of the CPI was imputed. It does not mean 37% of anything was guessed. A rise in
the different-cell share means the donor pools got thinner — BLS more often had to reach outside the
local area to find comparable observed price changes — which is a statement about *imputation quality*,
not *imputation quantity*.

The quantity comes from a different table (response rates), and even there the honest sentence is:

> In July 2026, **34.7%** of targeted Commodities & Services price quotes had no collected price, and
> their price change was imputed from observed price changes of similar items. In 2019 that figure
> averaged 21.0%.

Still with three caveats: it is an **unweighted count of price quotes**, not a share of the index by
expenditure weight; it **excludes** items priced from non-survey/transaction data, which is a growing
share of collection; and an imputed quote **borrows a measured price change**, it does not invent a
price level.

There is no number in any of these sources that supports "X% of the CPI is guessed." Do not publish one.

## What each number means

| Metric | Means | Does **not** mean |
|---|---|---|
| `cs_different_cell` | % **of C&S imputations** using a different-cell donor pool | % of the index imputed |
| `cs_imputed_share` | % of **targeted C&S quotes** with no collected price (BLS "percent non-responding") | % of index weight imputed; it covers probability-sample quotes only, and slightly **over**-states imputation — BLS notes responding + imputed quotes do not sum to 100%, most often because some quotes were initiated but not yet priced |
| `cs_quote_collection_rate` | collected quotes / targeted quotes | — |
| `cs_quote_estimation_rate` | quotes used in estimation / targeted quotes. Per BLS this numerator *adds* carry-forward and substitution quotes and *removes* rejected quotes; home-cell and different-cell imputations are **not** counted in it | a cleaner version of the collection rate |
| `cs_outlet_collection_rate` | % of targeted outlets (stores/sites) that gave any price | — |
| `housing_noninterview` | % **of housing imputations** that were non-interviews (vs vacancy) | % of rents imputed |
| `housing_imputed_share` | (vacant + other) / (reported + vacant + other) of targeted rent units. **Derived by P5**; BLS publishes the three components and they sum exactly to "total used in estimation" (verified each run, max abs diff 0.0000 pp) | % of the shelter index fabricated — imputed units borrow measured rent changes from comparable units |
| `modes_*` | % distribution of personal visit / telephone / online among collected prices | a quality ranking of modes |
| `p5_se` | BLS variance tables: **annual** median standard error of the published price change, by item and horizon | a monthly series; a *total* error — it is sampling error only, and does **not** cover nonresponse bias |
| `p5_stress` composite | an indicator of how far the collection environment has moved from 2019 | an error bar, a bias estimate, or a correction to the CPI |

**October 2025 is blank in every monthly source** (lapse in appropriations). P5 omits it. It is never
interpolated and never carried forward.

## The instrument stress indicator

An **indicator, not a measure**. Four monthly components, each z-scored against its own Jan–Dec 2019
mean and standard deviation, all oriented so higher = more of the index resting on imputation:

| domain | component | what it captures |
|---|---|---|
| C&S | `cs_different_cell` | imputation *quality* (donor pool had to widen) |
| C&S | `cs_imputed_share` | imputation *quantity* (quotes not collected) |
| Housing | `housing_noninterview` | imputation *quality* (non-interview vs vacancy mix) |
| Housing | `housing_imputed_share` | imputation *quantity* (units with imputed rent) |

`composite` = mean of the two **domain** scores, each domain score being the mean of its available
component z's. Domain-balancing means C&S and Housing each get half the weight no matter how many of
their components survived the fetch, and the series degrades gracefully if a source is down.

### Read the magnitude carefully

2019 was a very flat year: the component standard deviations are 0.83–1.19 pp, and the imputation
shares are published as **whole percents**, so part of that SD is just rounding. Dividing a ~15 pp move
by a ~1 pp baseline SD gives z-scores in the teens and thirties. **Those are not sigmas in any
probability sense** — the series are trending and autocorrelated, so a Gaussian tail reading is
meaningless. Read the shape and the ordering.

For that reason every row also carries `pp` (percentage points above the 2019 mean) and the composite
carries `composite_pp` — the domain-balanced average in percentage points. **`composite_pp` is the
number to put in front of a human.**

Standard errors are deliberately **not** in the composite: they are published annually and cannot be
z-scored against a 12-month 2019 baseline. They are reported separately as a ratio vs 2019.

### The composite so far

| month | z | pp above 2019 | context |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019-06 | 0.0 | 0.0 | baseline |
| 2020-04 | 12.0 | +10.8 | COVID collection shutdown |
| 2021-06 | 8.5 | +7.7 | partial recovery |
| 2023-06 | 4.7 | +4.3 | closest return to normal |
| 2025-05 | 14.6 | +13.8 | after the April-2025 suspensions |
| 2025-06 | 17.6 | +16.8 | collection-reduction notice month |
| 2025-12 | 20.2 | +19.1 | post-shutdown peak |
| 2026-07 | 18.1 | +16.9 | latest |

The series never returned to its 2019 level after COVID, and the 2025 level is materially above the
COVID peak.

| | Jul 2026 | 2019 mean | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| C&S imputations using a **different cell** | 37.0% | 7.8% | +29.2 pp |
| C&S targeted quotes **not collected** | 34.7% | 21.0% | +13.7 pp |
| C&S quote collection rate | 65.3% | 79.0% | −13.7 pp |
| C&S **outlet** collection rate | 73.5% | 87.7% | −14.2 pp |
| Rent units with **imputed rent** | 47.3% | 32.3% | +15.1 pp |
| Housing imputations that were **non-interviews** | 92.0% | 82.2% | +9.8 pp |
| Rent data **reported** by the tenant/landlord | 49.5% | 57.8% | −8.3 pp |
| Targeted C&S quotes (count) | 82,684 | 93,093 | −11% |
| Collected C&S quotes (count) | 53,966 | 73,536 | −27% |
| Targeted rent units (count) | 6,401 | 6,739 | −5% |

Note the sample-size line: **part of the collection-rate decline is by design.** BLS cut the collection
sample in 2025 (see the timeline), so targeted counts fall too. This is a budget/policy change, not
purely a wave of refusals.

### Standard errors have barely moved

Median standard error of the 12-month price change, U.S. city average (annual):

| year | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All items | 0.08 | 0.09 | 0.09 | 0.12 | 0.10 | 0.11 | **0.09** |
| All items less food and energy | 0.09 | 0.11 | 0.11 | 0.13 | 0.12 | 0.14 | **0.11** |
| Housing | 0.13 | 0.13 | 0.12 | 0.22 | 0.18 | 0.18 | **0.17** |

All-items SE inflation vs 2019 is **1.12×** — modest, and *below* the 2022 peak, even though imputation
roughly doubled. This is the most important nuance in P5: **the published standard errors measure
sampling error only.** The risk created by falling response is *nonresponse bias*, which no BLS series
here quantifies and which would not show up in these SEs. "The SEs barely moved" is not evidence that
nothing happened; it is evidence that the SEs are not measuring this.

## Event timeline (`p5_events`, from the BLS CPI notices index)

92 notices scraped, 2017-10-24 → 2026-07-27, auto-categorized; `impact=1` flags collection/shutdown/
COVID/data-quality events. The ones that matter for reading the series:

| date | category | notice |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-27 | methodology | 2028 Geographic Revision Notice |
| 2026-04-28 | data_quality | BLS engaged expert panels (NABE/CNSTAT) on **missing Consumer Expenditure data** (Oct–Nov 2025) and its impact on the CPI |
| 2026-05-12 | methodology | Rebasing of selected CPI series |
| 2025-12-17 | shutdown | Q&A on the 2025 federal government shutdown impact on the CPI |
| 2025-07-29 | collection | More information on CPI collection reductions |
| 2025-06-16 | collection | Notice of CPI collection reductions — pricing **suspended in Lincoln NE and Provo UT (April 2025)** and **Buffalo NY (June 2025)**, with a sample reduction elsewhere |
| 2020-04-10 → 2020-12-04 | covid | COVID-19 impact statements on CPI data |
| 2019-01-30 | shutdown | Partial government shutdown Q&A |

The categorizer is a keyword map (`CATS` in the pipeline); "other" is a genuine miscellany (webpage
redesigns, early releases). Re-read the notice itself before quoting one — P5 stores title, date and URL
only, never the body.

## Sources

| what | URL | cadence | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imputation source distribution (**primary**) | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/imputation-source-201901-present.xlsx | monthly, 2019-01→2026-07 | parsed |
| Imputation explainer / definitions | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/imputation.htm | — | read (not scraped) |
| Monthly response rates | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/response-rates/monthly-response-rates-201901-present.xlsx | monthly, 2019-01→2026-07 | parsed |
| Response-rate definitions | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/response-rates/home.htm | — | read (not scraped) |
| Collection modes | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/collection-modes-201901-present.xlsx | monthly, **2019-01→2025-11 (lags)** | parsed |
| Variance estimates (annual) | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/variance-estimates/home.htm → `<year>.xlsx`, 2019 is `2019.pdf` | annual, 2019→2025 | parsed (2019 via pdfplumber) |
| CPI notices index | https://www.bls.gov/cpi/notices/ | ad hoc | parsed |

Every fetch goes through `common.fetch` (BLS 403s without its User-Agent; Last-Modified cached). Any
source may fail without taking down the run — the failure is logged and recorded in the JSON's
`sources[]` with `parsed:false`, and the pipeline only exits non-zero if the **primary** imputation
XLSX fails.

Two things to watch: the collection-modes workbook currently lags the others by eight months, and the
variance year list is scraped off the page (not guessed), so a new annual file appears automatically.

## Tables

```
p5_imputation(month, survey, source, pct)                     -- distribution AMONG imputations
p5_response(month, metric, value, unit, source)               -- long; unit 'percent' 0-100 or 'count'
p5_mode(month, survey, mode, pct)
p5_se(month, item, horizon, se, median_change, period_type)   -- ANNUAL; month='<year>-12'
p5_events(date, title, url, category, impact)
p5_stress(month, component, z, pp, composite, composite_pp)   -- component '_composite' = the headline row
```

All are rebuilt from source on every run (idempotent). If the schema changes, `migrate()` drops and
recreates the affected table rather than silently keeping an old shape.
